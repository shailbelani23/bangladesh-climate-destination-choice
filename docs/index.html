Climate mobility in Bangladesh

The river took their land.
Where did they go?

An anonymized household moved from Faridpur to Manikganj after river erosion. This project asks why that destination became the observed choice among 64 districts.

One household illustration. The survey does not reveal a name or full biography, and the model does not reconstruct private reasoning.

What we know about the person

Loss. The household reported losing land or homestead land to river erosion.

Move. The household head reported moving from Faridpur to Manikganj in 2010.

Choice. The model compared Manikganj with every other district using information available for all candidates.

We do not know the household's name, emotions, finances, or private discussion. The story stays inside the facts the survey records.

What the model added

7.0%

Probability gravity assigned to Manikganj using distance and population. It ranked sixth.

13.7%

Probability after adding flood history, built surface, urban access, and cropland. It ranked second.

The model made the household's observed destination more plausible. It did not prove why the household moved or why Manikganj won the private decision.

The result repeats in two surveys

Positive values mean the GIS model assigned more probability to observed destinations than gravity did.

Where the model travels, and where it does not

Destination ranking transfers nationally. Predicting whether a climate-affected household stays within an unseen origin district requires more local information.
