# Figure captions and alt text

## Figure 1. Cross-dataset GIS gain

**Caption.** Mean out-of-sample log-loss improvement from adding four district GIS characteristics to the fitted gravity model. Positive values favor GIS. Whiskers are paired 95% household-cluster bootstrap intervals from 5,000 replicates. The BEMP interdistrict interval nearly touches zero; the corresponding BIHS erosion interval is positive.

**Alt text.** Horizontal forest plot with eight estimates. Every point lies to the right of zero. Full-choice gains are 0.108 for BEMP shock-linked moves, 0.098 for BIHS erosion moves, 0.058 for all BIHS household moves, and 0.108 for national BIHS migrants. Interdistrict gains are larger for the smaller household-relocation samples and 0.108 for national migrants. The BEMP interdistrict interval spans slightly below zero; the other seven intervals shown are above zero.

## Figure 2. Validation and transport

**Caption.** GIS log-loss improvement over gravity under household-grouped, leave-one-origin-out, and temporal validation. National BIHS migrant gains remain near 0.1 under all three tests. The full-choice climate estimates turn negative when an entire origin is unseen, while their interdistrict estimates remain positive.

**Alt text.** Dot plot with six sample and choice-set rows. Teal grouped-validation marks are positive for every row. Orange origin-held-out marks are negative for BEMP full choice and slightly negative for BIHS erosion full choice, but positive for both interdistrict climate rows and both national migrant rows. Purple temporal marks appear for the two national migrant rows near 0.1.

## Figure 3. One anonymized household move

**Caption.** An anonymized BIHS household head reported moving from Faridpur to Manikganj in 2010 after river erosion caused the loss of land or homestead land. Gravity assigned the observed destination 7.0% probability and rank 6 of 64. Adding GIS raised it to 13.7% and rank 2. The illustration describes an out-of-fold prediction, not a reconstruction of the household's private decision.

**Alt text.** Map of Bangladesh with Faridpur highlighted in orange and Manikganj in teal, joined by a short northeast arrow. Three labels read Loss, Move, and Choice. Horizontal bars show the probability assigned to Manikganj rising from 7.0% under gravity to 13.7% under gravity plus GIS, while its rank rises from sixth to second.
